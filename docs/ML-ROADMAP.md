# Machine learning for Snowmapper – options, data, volumes

Written 19.11.2026. Builds on `PLAN-neuschnee-schneetyp.md`; read that first.
Volumes are order-of-magnitude estimates; "effective samples" accounts for the
fact that neighbouring 1 km cells on the same day are not independent
(snowfall is correlated over ~20–30 km).

## 0 · Ground rule

ML does not fix a wrong input. The SLF and IMIS checks show ~2–3× too much
water reaching both layers. Fix that first (plan step A1): a model trained
on top of a broken input learns the bug, and has to be retrained once the bug is gone.

## 2 · Approaches – from cheap to bold

### 2.1 MOS: learn the correction, not the snow (quick win)
Gradient boosting (LightGBM/XGBoost) predicts the factor *measured / modelled*
for new snow per cell and day. Features: model precipitation, phase, SLR,
temperature, humidity, wind at 10 m / 850 hPa, height, aspect, slope, region,
lead time, month. Target: SLF HN1D, IMIS HS gain.
- **Data:** start with 1 winter (~150 snow days, ~5 000 effective samples);
  good with 2–3 winters.
- **Catch:** needs the model's *past forecasts*. They are not kept today, so
  either archive them daily from now on (see §4) or re-run Snowmapper on
  archived ICON forecasts.
- **Compute:** minutes on a laptop.
- **Bonus:** quantile regression gives "P(≥ 20 cm)" instead of one number.

### 2.2 Differentiable physics: keep the formula, learn its numbers
Write the new-snow chain (phase → SLR → altitude/luv/wind factors) in
PyTorch/JAX and fit its ~20–50 parameters jointly against SLF maps, IMIS and
rain gauges by gradient descent. Optionally replace one piece by a tiny network,
e.g. SLR(T, RH, wind). Stays explainable, cannot produce nonsense outside the data.
- **Data:** 50–100 snowfall events (≈2 winters) are enough for tens of parameters.
- This is `calibrate_newsnow.py` grown up: gradient descent instead of grid search.

### 2.3 Rain/snow phase from temperature and humidity
Label precipitation hours at stations as rain / snow / mixed (precipitation
together with a snow-depth increase, or observed present weather). Fit a
logistic model or a small tree model.
- **Data:** ~10⁴ labelled precipitation hours (≈50 stations × 5 winters).
  Zero-effort start: the published Jennings et al. (2018) T/RH curve.

### 2.4 New-snow density (SLR) model
Target: new-snow height and its water equivalent where observers measure both.
Features: temperature, humidity, wind, height, precipitation rate.
- **Data:** 2 000–10 000 observations.

### 2.5 Precipitation downscaling U-Net
Input ICON-CH2/CH1 precipitation and terrain → target CombiPrecip
(radar + gauges, 1 km hourly). Corrects the precipitation field before
anything else happens – the root of the current error.
- **Data:** 2–5 years of hourly fields with precipitation (~10⁴ hours),
  ~10–20 GB. **Compute:** 1 GPU, about a day.

### 2.6 Snow surface classifier (the snow-type layer)
Labels: gipfelbuch "Oben/Unten" (+ elevation of summit and start), app reports,
profiles' top layer. Features: Variant A's top 10–30 cm (density, grain type,
hardness, liquid water), hours since last snowfall, max temperature 24 h,
radiation by aspect, wind history, melt–freeze cycles.
- First a gradient-boosting model; later a sequence model (1D-CNN/GRU over the
  last 7 days of forcing) that predicts the surface directly.
- **Data:** at least ~200 labels per class, ~1 500 in total, to start; 5 000–10 000
  for a robust model (reports are noisy). Historic gipfelbuch reports
  (3–5 winters) could cover this in one go – with your login, slowly, and
  after checking the site's terms.
- **Catch:** features need Variant A runs for those dates → season replay.

### 2.7 LLM label extraction (makes 2.6 much better)
Regex finds "Schnee ab 1800 m" but misses "unten noch braun, oben ab dem
Sattel durchgehend weiss". An LLM turns each report into a strict JSON:
surface per elevation band and aspect, snow line, new snow cm, wind effects,
crust thickness, confidence. Validate on ~200 hand-checked reports.
- **Data:** none extra – better labels from the same reports.
  **Cost:** small per report; one batch run over a few thousand reports.

### 2.8 SNOWPACK emulator → snow types at 250 m or 50 m (the bold one)
Variant A runs SNOWPACK at ~135 weather points × virtual slopes and
interpolates. A neural network (LSTM/Transformer/temporal CNN) trained on
SNOWPACK's own output – forcing sequence in, HS + top-layer density, grain
class, liquid water out – runs in milliseconds per cell. That makes a
layered snowpack *per terrain cell* at 250 m (or 50 m) affordable.
- **Data:** synthetic, unlimited: 10⁴–10⁵ slope-seasons of SNOWPACK runs
  (~10–50 GB hourly). **Compute to generate:** ~10–15 CPU-days (about 10 s
  per slope-season); **to train:** 1 GPU, 1–3 days.
- Validated by IMIS (does the emulator still beat the station check?) and by
  the surface labels of 2.6.

### 2.9 Data assimilation with satellite and stations
Ensemble Kalman or particle filter on the snowpack state: IMIS HS (point),
Sentinel-2 snow cover (where is snow at all), Sentinel-1 wet snow (melt
onset). ML enters as the *observation operator*, e.g. modelled HS + terrain
→ predicted snow-cover fraction at 20 m.
- **Data:** per winter ~60–80 Sentinel-2 scenes, 30 Sentinel-1 passes, IMIS.

### 2.10 Webcams as snow-line and new-snow sensors
Fine-tune a pretrained segmentation network on webcam images (snow / no
snow / sky), project onto the DEM → snow line per valley, hourly.
- **Data:** 2 000–5 000 annotated images, 10⁵–10⁶ unlabelled; 1 GPU day.
  Creative but labour-intensive; do it last.

### 2.11 Weak labels from the avalanche bulletin
"Wind slab, aspects NW–N–E above 2200 m" per region and day is a label for
the wind layer; "wet snow" for the wet classes. Daily since years, no labelling cost.
- **Data:** ~25 winters × ~150 regions × ~150 days, but coarse; use as a
  sanity score, not as the only target.

### Not worth it (now)
Training your own AI weather model (GraphCast/AIFS-type): needs decades of
reanalysis and GPU clusters. Use ICON-CH and fix the post-processing instead.

## 4 · Start collecting now (costs nothing, unlocks 2.1/2.2/2.6)

1. **Archive the model's past forecasts daily.** Per day the 24 h sums of
   PREC, SNOW and phase-weighted precipitation plus mean T/RH/wind on the 1 km
   grid, per lead time (0–5 days): ~2–5 MB/day compressed, ~1 GB/winter. Without
   this, every ML model has to wait for re-runs.
2. **Store Variant A's surface metrics** at every gipfelbuch/app report location
   and time (top-layer density, grain, LWC, wind index), so the surface classifier
   gets features without a replay.
3. **Backfill the SLF maps since 11/2022** and historic gipfelbuch reports
   (with login, slowly).
4. Request **IMIS and observer history** from the SLF data service / EnviDat.

## 5 · Recommended order

1. Fix the precipitation input (no ML).
2. 2.2 + 2.3 + 2.4: physics with learned numbers – explainable, small data.
3. §4 archiving from day one; 2.7 LLM labels on historic gipfelbuch reports.
4. 2.1 MOS once one winter of archived forecasts exists.
5. 2.6 surface classifier once ~1 500 labels exist.
6. 2.8 SNOWPACK emulator → per-cell snow types at 250 m.
7. 2.5, 2.9, 2.10 as research projects.
